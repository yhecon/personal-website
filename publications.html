2025JEEM

Air Pollution and the Airborne Diseases: Evidence from China and Japan

Journal of Environmental Economics and Management, 2025.

With Guojun He and Takanao Tanaka.

Presented at the 2nd JEEM Conference, GoSee Webinar, 15th Applied Econometrics Conference, and Renmin University.

Read abstract

This paper estimates how ambient air pollution affects COVID-19 and influenza. It combines an epidemiological SIRD framework with instrumental variables and flexible distributed lags. Evidence from China's first COVID-19 wave indicates that pollution significantly increased the daily growth rate of COVID-19, while estimates for influenza healthcare visits in Japan are small and statistically insignificant.

2020JEEM

Mitigating the Air Pollution Effect? The Remarkable Decline in the Pollution-Mortality Relationship in Hong Kong

Journal of Environmental Economics and Management, 2020.

With Guojun He and Chun Wai Cheung.

Read abstract

Using sixteen years of mortality data and transboundary pollution from mainland China as an instrument, this study estimates the causal effect of air pollution on mortality in Hong Kong. A ten-unit increase in the Air Pollution Index raises monthly cardio-respiratory mortality by 1.77%, with especially large effects among older people. The relationship declines sharply after the 2003 SARS epidemic, consistent with improved emergency care and community health services reducing short-run vulnerability.

Reducing Single-Use Cutlery with Green Nudges: Evidence from China's Food-Delivery Industry

Science, 2023.

Guojun He, Yuhang Pan†, Albert Park, Yasuyuki Sawada, and Elaine S. Tan.

Read abstract

Using customer-level data from Eleme, this study evaluates two green nudges: making “no cutlery” the default and rewarding the choice with green points. The intervention increased the share of no-cutlery orders by 648%. If adopted nationwide, the estimated annual savings exceed 21.75 billion sets of cutlery, 3.26 million metric tons of plastic waste, and 5.44 million trees.

2022Nature HB

Short- and Medium-Term Impacts of Strict Anti-Contagion Policies on Non-COVID-19 Mortality in China

Nature Human Behaviour, 2022.

Jinlei Qi, Dandan Zhang, Xiang Zhang, Takanao Tanaka, Yuhang Pan, Peng Yin, Jiangmei Liu, Shuocen Liu, George F. Gao, Guojun He, and Maigeng Zhou.

Read abstract

Using death registries covering 300 million people and a difference-in-differences design, the study finds that China's strict anti-contagion policies reduced non-COVID-19 mortality outside Wuhan by 4.6%. The reduction grew to 12.5% after the measures were relaxed. Changes in protective behaviour, lower air pollution, and fewer traffic accidents may explain these substantial unintended health benefits.

2020Nature Sust.

The Short-Term Impacts of COVID-19 Lockdown on Urban Air Pollution in China

Nature Sustainability, 2020.

Guojun He, Yuhang Pan, and Takanao Tanaka.

Read abstract

Comparing Chinese cities with and without formal lockdowns, this study finds that lockdowns reduced the Air Quality Index by 19.84 points and PM2.5 by 14.07 μg/m³. Other nationwide anti-contagion measures also improved air quality in cities without lockdowns. Pollution nevertheless remained far above health guidelines, and conventional environmental regulation could achieve similar gains at much lower economic cost.

Particulate Air Pollution and Mortality in 38 of China's Largest Cities: Time-Series Analysis

The BMJ, 2017.

Peng Yin, Guojun He, Maoyong Fan, Kwok Yan Chiu, Maorong Fan, Chang Liu, An Xue, Tong Liu, Yuhang Pan, Quan Mu, and Maigeng Zhou.

Read abstract

Based on 350,638 deaths across 38 large Chinese cities, the study estimates that a 10 μg/m³ increase in same-day PM10 is associated with a 0.44% rise in daily deaths. Effects are larger for cardiorespiratory mortality, women, and adults aged 60 or older, and vary substantially across cities.
